Purpose: identify what the current documents actually establish, where Dry1Out statements do not cleanly match those

BOTTOM LINE
• The strongest issue is not bad arithmetic. The major signed change-order totals appear internally consistent.
• The stronger problem is documentation and attribution: scope descriptions, payment demands, insurance-funded work, customerfunded upgrades, and delay explanations do not always reconcile cleanly.
• Dry1Out's paint description is materially narrower than the State Farm estimate. State Farm funds painting by measured surface and
coat; the reviewed estimate does not state a "one color only" requirement.
• Anne repeatedly asked for ordinary billing support before payment. The correspondence shows conflicting payment figures and 
responses that did not explain the derivation.
• There is real overlap exposure between insurance-funded plumbing/electrical/countertop work and later customer-funded upgrades. 
That is not proof of double billing, but it requires a line-by-line credit/reconciliation.

FIVE RECORDS THAT WOULD SETTLE MOST OF THIS
1. Paint authority: exact contract/estimate provision supporting a one-color or existing-color restriction.
2. Scope-credit crosswalk: State Farm-funded scope -> owner-requested upgrade -> credit for displaced insurance work -> 
incremental owner charge.
3. Payment ledger: every invoice, payment, wire/check, credit and current balance, with each payment applied to a specific 
contract/change-order line.
4. Backup for bid/pass-through charges: engineer proposal/invoice, hazardous-material testing/remediation support, and the Gurries 
Electrical sub-estimate referenced by State Farm.
5. Delay chronology: each claimed owner-caused delay, date, dependency, number of days, and actual impact on completion.
DO NOT OVERSTATE
• Do not claim the signed change-order arithmetic is fraudulent. The totals reviewed reconcile internally.
• Do not call the plumbing/electrical/countertop overlap "double billing" until credits and final invoicing are reconciled.
• Do not treat "hazardous material remediation" as asbestos work without supporting records.
• Do not rely on the reported CSLB suspension until the current license record and effective dates are independently verified.
